Hello, I'm Carmen

An English-language educator, instructional designer, and independent publisher in Irvine, California. Years of experience in language education shaped what I look for in good material: clear instructions, a realistic time frame, and an obvious purpose behind every task. That is the standard I write to.

The short version

  • 2015 to 2026, Education First. Started as an online instructor, moved through the young-learner and adult programs, and now work in the Teach Online program supporting freelance English instructors around the world.
  • Taught adults, a lot of them. General, business, and exam-prep English including TOEFL, IELTS, and TOEIC. Around twenty hours a week, forty to fifty regular students.
  • Regular lesson observations. Watching live teacher-taught lessons against a rubric each month and writing feedback they could use, plus supplying observation data to train an AI lesson-review tool.
  • Peer review of course builds. Reviewed colleagues' courses in Articulate Rise and on the LMS, with feedback on structure, clarity, and level fit.
  • Courses, job aids, and help articles. Self-paced courses, refresher sheets, and support articles, revised based on what completion and usage data showed.
  • Mentoring. New instructors through their first three months, and six academic coordinators and managers during a department expansion.
  • Nearly seven years in academic management. Academic Manager since 2019, owning academic quality for a network of over one hundred instructors, setting standards, running onboarding and training through platform rollouts, and writing the procedures the team worked from.

What the teaching years taught me

Teaching at volume, across many first languages and starting levels, is where the useful lessons landed. You find out quickly what works on the page and what falls apart the second a real learner reads it. Observations added the other half: watching teachers deliver live lessons, then writing feedback they could act on the following week. I have also reviewed colleagues' courses in Rise and on our LMS, giving feedback on structure, clarity, level fit, and whether a learner could follow the sequence without help.

How a sample gets made

  • Needs analysis. Who the learner is, what they are trying to do, and where the language gets in the way.
  • Brief, then draft. A short plan before any writing starts.
  • Level check. Task demand and language checked against the provided descriptors (K-12 core competencies, CEFR levels, etc).
  • Revision. For clarity, sequencing, and layout, until it reads cleanly the first time.
  • Two versions together. Student and teacher, with answer keys, timing, and staging notes where a teacher needs them.

Where AI fits, and where it does not

All work on this site is human owned. The writing and the design decisions are mine, and I am accountable for what appears here. AI acts as a review assistant while I draft, giving feedback on clarity, level fit, consistency, and anything I may have left out. It is never the author and never the last step. Everything passes through my own review and revision before it is published here or sent to an editor. For the simple teaching websites and interactive activities under Sample Websites, the concept and learning design are mine, AI builds them to my specifications, and I test and revise them afterward.
